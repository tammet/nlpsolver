You decide whether one open name implies another.

You are given pairs of names taken from a single short passage that has been
translated into logic. Each name is written in lowercase with underscores and
was built from the words of the passage itself. For each pair you see:

  - the two names, A and B, and their kind (concept or relation);
  - one example atom for each, showing the argument positions and whether that
    occurrence was negated;
  - the passage sentence or sentences in which each name occurs.

A name may come from anywhere in the case. You are NOT told what the question
of the case is, and you must not try to guess it. Judge the pair on its own
meaning and on the sentences you are shown.


== WHAT THE LABELS MEAN ==

For a concept pair, "A implies B" means: for every thing X, if X is an A then
X is a B.

    A_IMPLIES_B                 isa(A,X)  ->  isa(B,X)
    B_IMPLIES_A                 isa(B,X)  ->  isa(A,X)
    EQUIVALENT                  both directions hold
    A_IMPLIES_NOT_B             isa(A,X)  ->  NOT isa(B,X)
    B_IMPLIES_NOT_A             isa(B,X)  ->  NOT isa(A,X)
    EXCLUSIVE                   nothing can be both an A and a B
    RELATED_BUT_NO_IMPLICATION  the two are about the same subject matter but
                                neither direction holds in general
    UNRELATED                   the two have nothing to do with each other
    UNCERTAIN                   you cannot decide, or it depends on a reading
                                the sentences do not fix

For a relation pair, "A implies B" means: for all X and Y, if A holds between
X and Y then B holds between X and Y, IN THE ARGUMENT ORDER SHOWN.

    A_IMPLIES_B                 A(X,Y)  ->  B(X,Y)
    INVERSE                     A(X,Y)  ->  B(Y,X), and B(X,Y) -> A(Y,X)

INVERSE is for relation pairs only. Use it when the two names describe the same
situation from the two ends ("parent_of" and "child_of", "contains" and
"inside_of"). If A and B hold in the same argument order, that is A_IMPLIES_B
or EQUIVALENT, not INVERSE.

Some pairs show two atoms with a constant in one argument
("study(X, art_history)" and "gain(X, inspiration)"). There, "A implies B"
means: for every X, if the first atom holds of X then the second holds of X.
Judge exactly the two atoms as printed, constants included.

Some pairs ask about a constant pair with the two atoms that use them
("requires(X, labeled_data)" and "requires(X, data)"). There you are deciding
whether every thing of the first kind is also a thing of the second kind, in
the sense the two atoms use them. Note the direction carefully: a requirement
is not monotone in the same direction as a possession.


== HOW TO DECIDE ==

1. Read the sentences. A passage often defines a name where it introduces it
   ("Reflection is when a wave bounces off a surface"); a definition licenses
   an implication, an example does not.
2. Prefer the weaker label. If A implies B only usually or only in this
   passage's setting, that is still A_IMPLIES_B — the rules built from your
   labels are defeasible. But if A implies B only under a further condition
   that neither name carries, answer RELATED_BUT_NO_IMPLICATION.
3. Do not upgrade a "some" to an "every". If the sentences say that some A are
   B, that is not A_IMPLIES_B.
4. Do not reverse a direction the sentences state. If a sentence says every B
   is an A, the pair is B_IMPLIES_A even when the reverse sounds plausible.
5. Quantifier words inside a name are part of it: "loves_some_animal" and
   "loves_every_animal" are different names and neither implies the other in
   the loose direction.
6. Answer UNCERTAIN rather than guessing. An UNCERTAIN pair is used more
   carefully than a wrong confident one.
7. Names with modality inside them ("can_swim", "must_pay") are about the
   modality, not the act: "swims" implies "can_swim", not the other way.


== WHERE YOUR ANSWER COMES FROM ==

Every label except UNRELATED carries one tag saying where you got it:

    LEXICAL     the two names are one predicate in other wording. Tense,
                number, a "has_" prefix, "form_of_", a different word order,
                a noun beside its adjective: "runs" and "is_running",
                "energy" and "form_of_energy", "colour_of" and "has_colour".
    STATED      a SINGLE passage sentence you were shown says it directly.
                One sentence defines one name in terms of the other, or
                asserts the implication outright. If you have to combine two
                sentences, or read a "some" as an "all", or take one step
                through something you know yourself, it is NOT STATED.
    BACKGROUND  you know it from outside the passage. Nothing you were shown
                says it; it is true of the world.

Tag the source of the implication, not your certainty about it. A well-known
fact the passage never mentions is BACKGROUND even when you are sure of it.
A sentence that gives one example is not STATED — see rule 3 above.

Then give your confidence in the label itself:

    HIGH        you would be surprised to be wrong
    MEDIUM      the label is right on the reading you took
    LOW         you leaned one way but the case is thin

UNCERTAIN carries a confidence too: it says how sure you are that the pair
cannot be decided, not how sure you are of a direction.


== OUTPUT FORMAT ==

One line per pair, in the order the pairs are given, and nothing else except
optional WHY lines. The label comes first, then the tag, then the confidence,
each in square brackets:

PAIR 1: A_IMPLIES_B [LEXICAL] [HIGH]
PAIR 2: UNRELATED
PAIR 3: EXCLUSIVE [BACKGROUND] [HIGH]
WHY: nothing is both a city and a state.
PAIR 4: UNCERTAIN [BACKGROUND] [MEDIUM]
WHY: "conductive" may mean thermal or electrical here.
PAIR 5: B_IMPLIES_A [STATED] [HIGH]

UNRELATED takes no tag and no confidence. Every other label takes both.

A WHY line is optional, at most one line, and only after a negative label
(A_IMPLIES_NOT_B, B_IMPLIES_NOT_A, EXCLUSIVE) or UNCERTAIN. For a negative
label the WHY line should say why nothing can be both.

Give a line for every pair. Do not add commentary, headings or blank
explanations. Do not restate the pair.
