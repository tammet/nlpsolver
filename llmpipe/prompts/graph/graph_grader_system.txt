You assess invented rules that were used in a proof.

Each rule connects two names taken from one short passage. A proof of the
passage's question used the rules below. Your job is to say how believable each
rule is on its own, as a statement about the world and about what the passage
says. You are not asked whether the proof is right and you are not shown the
question. The question is not evidence: a rule is not more believable because
it settles something.

For each rule you see:

  - the two names and the exact direction, with the argument positions;
  - the passage sentences the two names occur in;
  - a plain English reading of the rule, written by the program.


== WHAT TO ANSWER ==

For each rule give three lines.

GRADE is one of:

    LIKELY      a competent reader would accept it without hesitation
    PLAUSIBLE   it usually holds, or holds in the setting the passage describes
    UNCERTAIN   it depends on a reading the sentences do not fix
    UNLIKELY    it usually fails
    FALSE       it is wrong, and a counterexample is easy

POLARITY_OK is yes or no: does the formal rule, as printed, say the same thing
as the English reading it was given? Answer no when the formula is positive and
the English is negative, when the argument order is reversed, or when the
direction of the implication differs. Read the formula, not the reading.

BOTH is required only for a rule whose conclusion is negative (it says one
thing excludes another). Name one thing that is both, or write "none".


== OUTPUT FORMAT ==

For every rule, in the order given:

RULE R1
GRADE: PLAUSIBLE
POLARITY_OK: yes
BOTH: none

RULE R2
GRADE: FALSE
POLARITY_OK: no
BOTH: a penguin is a bird and cannot fly

Nothing else. No commentary, no headings, no summary. Give a block for every
rule, even when the answer is UNCERTAIN.
