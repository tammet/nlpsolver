Someone has proposed the rule below and provisionally accepted it. Your job is
to try to break it.

Assume it is wrong and look for the reason. Only if you genuinely cannot find
one should you let it pass. You are not told the problem's answer, and nothing
has been derived from this rule that you should try to protect; a rule that
would be convenient is not thereby correct.

One thing is NOT a fault: that the passage does not state the connection. A
bridge may rest on what the words themselves mean — a kind-of relation, a
compound and its head, an event and its conventional noun — and such a bridge is
sound even though the passage never spells it out. "The text never says this" is
therefore not a finding. What you must decide is what the connection rests on,
and then whether the direction, sign, arguments, quantification and scope are
right given that.

Some of the wording comes from the question being asked. A question tells you
which expression and sense is at issue. It is not evidence that the expression
is true, false or denied, and its polarity is not a counterexample. "Is no A a
B?" does not say that no A is a B; it asks.

HOW TO READ THE LOGIC

These are the notation's conventions.  They are facts about the notation, not
hints about this problem.

  isa(TYPE, THING)
      The FIRST argument names the type; the second is the individual or
      variable that is of that type.  `isa(bird, ?X)` says ?X is a bird.  It
      does NOT say bird is an ?X.

  is rel2(RELATION, X, Y)
      The first argument names the relation; X and Y are its two participants,
      in that order.  `is rel2(member of, ?X, C)` says ?X is a member of C.

  has property(PROPERTY, X)
      The first argument names the property; X is what has it.

  has degree property(PROPERTY, X, DEGREE, CLASS)
      As above, with a degree and the comparison class it belongs to.

  has type(E, WORD) / has actor(E, X) / has target(E, X)
      An event E, what kind it is, and who fills each role.

  forall ?X . forall ?Y . ( BODY -> normally HEAD )
      The rule fires for EVERY combination of ?X and ?Y that makes BODY true,
      and concludes HEAD for that combination.  It does NOT say that one thing
      stands in the relation to all of them.  `forall ?X . forall ?Y . (
      loves(?X, ?Y) & animal(?Y) -> normally animal_lover(?X) )` fires once for
      each pair where ?X loves an animal ?Y; it does not require ?X to love
      every animal.

  normally
      The conclusion holds by default and can be overridden by a contrary fact.
      It is not a claim of necessity.

  A name like "Tom 1" or a URL is one specific individual.  A `?X` is a
  variable: the rule speaks about everything that matches it.

Work through these six, in order, and say what you find under each:

1. DIRECTION AND SIGN
   Does the English license this direction, or only the converse? Does it
   license concluding the denial, or only the absence of a claim?

2. VARIABLES, CONSTANTS AND ARGUMENTS
   Is something quantified that the text only says of one named individual? Is
   something fixed to a name that the text says generally? Are the arguments in
   the positions the English puts them in — is it X about Y, or Y about X?

3. A MISSING CONDITION
   Does the English state the connection only under some circumstance that the
   rule does not require? Name it.

4. SCOPE
   The rule as written fires on everything matching its left side. Is that
   wider than what the text supports?

5. WHAT DOES IT REST ON
   Name the category from the list below.  Two ways of saying one thing, or a
   fact about the words, is fine.  Two different things that often go together
   is not, however reliable the correlation.  A denial inferred from the passage
   not mentioning something is not.

6. A COUNTEREXAMPLE
   Describe a concrete situation consistent with everything the text says, in
   which the left side holds and the conclusion does not. If you can build one,
   the rule is broken. If you try and cannot, say what you tried.  A question's
   wording is not a counterexample, and neither is the passage's silence.

WHAT THE CONNECTION RESTS ON

Say where the connection comes from, using exactly one of these names:

  TEXTUAL_REPRESENTATION
      The passage itself states or defines the connection, or the two sides are
      two renderings of one thing the passage says.
  LEXICAL_TAXONOMY
      The words themselves carry it: a kind-of relation, a compound and its
      head, a synonym.  This does NOT have to be stated in the passage.  That a
      sloop is a vessel is a fact about the words, and a passage that mentions
      a sloop without defining one has still used a vessel.
  CONVENTIONAL_NOMINALIZATION_OR_ROLE
      The same happening written two conventional ways: an event and its noun,
      or a relation and its converse naming.  Also not required to be stated.
  STABLE_BACKGROUND_RELATION
      Reliable knowledge about the world that is not a fact about the words —
      that ice forms below freezing, that a capital city is in its country.
      Real knowledge, but not a representation alignment.
  SPECULATIVE_CORRELATION
      Things that often go together, or a stereotype, or something that merely
      helps settle the question.
  OPEN_WORLD_NEGATIVE
      A denial inferred from the passage not saying otherwise.  "Nothing says
      X is a Y, so normally X is not a Y" is this, always.
  NONE
      No connection of any kind.

The first three are representation and lexical abstraction; they may be used
even when the passage does not assert them.  STABLE_BACKGROUND_RELATION is real
but is recorded and not used here.  The last three are never used.

Report what the connection ACTUALLY rests on.  Do not pick a stronger category
because the rule seems useful.

Then end with exactly these two lines and nothing after them:

FINAL: PASS | FAIL | UNCERTAIN
EVIDENCE: one of the evidence names above
FAULT: direction | sign | arguments | missing_guard | scope | counterexample | evidence_category | none

FAIL if you found a real fault. UNCERTAIN if you cannot decide. PASS only if you
attacked it under all six headings and it held. Use FAULT: evidence_category
when the rule's only support is a correlation, an open-world denial, or nothing.
Only these three lines are read.
