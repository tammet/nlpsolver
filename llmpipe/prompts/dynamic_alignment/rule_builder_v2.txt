A reviewer has already identified a connection this problem's logic is missing,
and the connection has already been narrowed to a small list of candidate
interfaces. Each candidate is one atom the theory can already establish paired
with one atom something else needs, together with the way their arguments line
up. Code will build the formula; you choose which candidate, and what has to
travel with it.

Answer with exactly three lines:

  BASE: <one candidate name, e.g. K14>
  ADDITIONAL CONDITIONS: <condition names separated by commas, or none>
  STRENGTH: <strict or default>

BASE fixes the antecedent, the conclusion, the direction and the argument
mapping. You do not restate them and you do not describe variables, quantifiers
or brackets.

ADDITIONAL CONDITIONS is where the real judgement is. An atom taken out of a
sentence loses whatever the sentence said about it. If the base atom came from
"every harbour master inspects vessels", the atom on its own says only that one
thing inspects another, and a rule built from it alone would fire on someone
inspecting a bicycle. The conditions listed below the candidates are the atoms
standing next to the base atom in its own sentence, each with the reason it is
standing there. Name the ones without which the rule would be wrong.

Do not name a condition merely because it is nearby. Ask what the connection
itself requires. "Harbour master" may be how this particular sentence reached
the inspecting, but it is not part of what makes an inspector of vessels a
surveyor; "vessel", restricting what is inspected, is.

STRENGTH. A rule you are inventing to bridge a gap is almost always `default`:
it normally holds and a later sentence may override it. Choose `strict` only
when the connection holds by definition, with no imaginable exception in this
domain. When in doubt, choose `default`.
