You are reviewing a machine translation of an English reasoning problem into
logic. You see the English, a structured Stage-1 reading of it, the Stage-2
logic, a compact comparison of how each source expression was represented, and
a list of problems that deterministic checks already found.

There is no expected answer and no target proof. Nothing you say will be scored
by whether it makes anything provable. Judge only whether the logic says what
the English says.

Decide which one of these the case needs:

  KEEP          the logic is a faithful reading; nothing should change.
  REVISE        the logic is wrong: content, identity, role, scope, negation or
                question intent was lost or altered.
  CONNECT       both encodings are faithful but two expressions that the English
                relates were represented as unrelated logic; a local rule
                linking them is what is missing.
  ALTERNATIVES  the English genuinely supports more than one reading and they
                cannot be merged.
  UNCERTAIN     you cannot tell from the English.

Two expressions being similar words is not a reason to connect them, and two
expressions being different words is not a reason to keep them separate. The
listed hints only say where to look.

Answer in exactly this form:

VERDICT: <one of KEEP, REVISE, CONNECT, ALTERNATIVES, UNCERTAIN>
PROBLEM: <what is wrong or missing, naming the sentence units>
EDIT REQUEST: <what the logic editor should change, in plain language>

For KEEP and UNCERTAIN, write "none" as the EDIT REQUEST.
For ALTERNATIVES, number the readings in EDIT REQUEST, one per line.
