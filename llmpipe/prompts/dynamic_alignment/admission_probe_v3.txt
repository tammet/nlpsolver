A translation pipeline turned a short passage into logic, could not answer the
question, and generated candidate rules to bridge the gap. You are shown several
candidates for the SAME connection and asked to assess each one.

Each candidate now comes with every way it was DERIVED. One formula can be
reached by more than one construction: a reader may have named its parts, or
code may have assembled it from the sentences on its own, and the same text can
arrive both ways. The derivations differ in how much anyone actually vouched for
them, so they are all shown and you are asked which of them, if any, you would
rest on.

YOU ARE NOT PICKING A WINNER

Two candidates can both be worth keeping for different reasons. A BROAD rule can
be reliable background knowledge even when the passage supports only a narrower
guarded version; the NARROW one can be what the passage licenses even when the
broad one is true. Keep both, at different levels, and say what each one is.

THREE JUDGMENTS

**Could it be true?** `semantic`:

  SUPPORTED     ordinary meaning or the passage clearly licenses it
  PLAUSIBLE     reliable background knowledge, not established by the passage
  SPECULATIVE   possibly useful over-abstraction, not reliably true
  UNSUPPORTED   nothing meaningful supports it
  INVALID       a contradiction, a broken scope, or another definite error

**Does this passage say it?** `passage`:

  PASSAGE_GENERAL     the passage licenses it in general
  PASSAGE_RESTRICTED  the passage licenses it only in the restricted case
  BACKGROUND_ONLY     true or reliable, but the passage does not establish it
  NOT_SUPPORTED       the passage gives no support
  UNCLEAR             you cannot tell

**Does the rule run the right way?** `direction`. This is a separate question
and the distinction inside it matters:

  AFFIRMED         you can say why the implication holds in this direction
  CONTEXT_ONLY     it holds for these particular things, not in general
  UNKNOWN          you have neither positive support NOR a concrete
                   counterexample — you simply do not know
  COUNTEREXAMPLED  you can state a case where it fails: "a wheel is part of a
                   car and is not a member of it"
  INCOHERENT       the participants are swapped, or the rule is corrupted

UNKNOWN and COUNTEREXAMPLED are not the same and must not be merged. An UNKNOWN
direction stays available as an exploratory hypothesis — untested is not
refuted. A COUNTEREXAMPLED direction is archived with your reason and never run.
Do not mark a direction COUNTEREXAMPLED unless you can name the counterexample,
and do not mark it AFFIRMED merely because the two words are related: `part of`
and `member of` are related and one direction of them fails.

WHERE THE CONNECTION COMES FROM

`evidence` is exactly one of: TEXTUAL_REPRESENTATION, LEXICAL_TAXONOMY,
CONVENTIONAL_NOMINALIZATION_OR_ROLE, STABLE_BACKGROUND_RELATION,
SPECULATIVE_CORRELATION, OPEN_WORLD_NEGATIVE, NONE.

WHAT THE GUARD DOES

`restriction` is one of: NO_RESTRICTION_NEEDED, RELEVANT_RESTRICTION_PRESENT,
RELEVANT_RESTRICTION_MISSING, IRRELEVANT_RESTRICTION, UNCLEAR.

A relevant guard makes a rule more faithful to the passage; it does not make the
unguarded rule false. A guard that restricts the wrong thing makes a rule
NARROWER without making it more faithful — do not credit it for being narrow.

WHICH DERIVATIONS YOU REST ON

Each candidate lists its derivation paths as P1, P2, … Each says what a reader
named, what code added, and whether code invented an identification between two
sentences — an UNVERIFIED attachment, which nothing in the logic established.

  supporting_paths   the paths you would rest the formula on
  uncertain_paths    paths you can neither rely on nor rule out

A path you list in neither is treated as one you do not support. Name paths only
by the ids printed for that candidate.

WHAT YOU MUST NOT DO

Do not ask whether a rule would let the prover reach an answer. You are not told
the answer and must not guess it.

HOW TO ANSWER

Reason in prose if you want. Then end with exactly one line per candidate, and
nothing after them:

ASSESS: A; semantic=PLAUSIBLE; evidence=STABLE_BACKGROUND_RELATION; passage=BACKGROUND_ONLY; restriction=NO_RESTRICTION_NEEDED; direction=UNKNOWN; supporting_paths=P2; uncertain_paths=P1; reason=the words plainly relate but I know of no case settling this direction

Use the candidate letters, the path ids and the value words exactly as printed.
Every candidate needs a line. Only those lines are read.
