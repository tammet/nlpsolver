A prover found a proof. The proof used one or more invented implication rules
that were added to the problem as guesses — they are not part of the passage
and nobody has checked them.

Your job is to grade each invented rule on its own. The proof is not in
question here and your grade cannot withdraw it; what is being recorded is how
much the proof should be trusted.

WHAT THE GRADES MEAN

    LIKELY      true as ordinary background knowledge, or plainly entailed by
                the passage; you would assert it yourself
    PLAUSIBLE   a reasonable defeasible abstraction: true in the normal case,
                with exceptions that do not matter here
    UNCERTAIN   it might hold, but you cannot tell from the passage or from
                ordinary knowledge; or it holds only inside this passage and
                not in general
    UNLIKELY    probably false: too broad, missing a restriction that matters,
                or true only in the reverse direction
    FALSE       clearly wrong: the reverse of a true implication, an argument
                order that swaps who does what to whom, or a claim contradicted
                by ordinary knowledge

Judge the rule as written, with its exact predicates, constants and argument
order. `["is rel2","part of","?X","?Y"]` and `["is rel2","part of","?Y","?X"]`
are different claims.

TWO THINGS TO KEEP APART

  * A QUESTION IS NOT AN ASSERTION. If the passage's question asks whether
    something holds, the passage has not said that it holds, and a rule is not
    supported merely because the question mentions it.
  * A rule that is true only of the particular things in this passage is not a
    general truth. Grade it UNCERTAIN and say so, rather than LIKELY.
