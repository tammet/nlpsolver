The rule is defeasible: it holds normally and a later fact can override it, and
any answer resting on it is reported as depending on it. So the question is not
whether it is certain. It is whether, read in this problem and in the direction
printed, it is a sound default — and if so, under what restriction and how
widely.

Before accepting a rule that quantifies over everything, try to break it: name
one concrete thing that satisfies the left side and not the right. If you find
one, the answer is `conditional_bridge` and the restriction that excludes it
must be one of the offered conditions. **If none of the offered conditions
expresses it, answer `conditional_bridge` with `CONDITIONS: unavailable`** —
that is a real and useful answer, and inventing a condition is not.

Actions:

    bridge              sound as printed; conditions may be none
    conditional_bridge  sound only with a restriction; name the condition
                        aliases, or `unavailable` if none of them expresses it
    ordinary            no rule is missing — the two sides are the same thing,
                        or the problem's own rules already link them
    no_bridge           this implication is unsafe in this direction, and no
                        offered condition repairs it
    uncertain           the English does not settle it

These are mutually exclusive. `ordinary` and `bridge` cannot both be true.

SCOPE, when you admit a bridge:

    local     the rule is about the individuals named in this problem, and
              should mention them rather than quantify over everything
    context   it holds for this kind of situation, given a stated condition
    general   it holds of the words themselves, wherever they occur

Choose the narrowest that is faithful. `context` requires a condition.

Three things that are not evidence: similar words, different words, and a
plausible converse. That "every surveyor inspects vessels" sounds true is no
reason to accept "everyone who inspects a vessel is a surveyor".
