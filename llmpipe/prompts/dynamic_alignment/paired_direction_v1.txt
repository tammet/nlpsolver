Code built two rules that are exact converses of each other: each one's
conclusion is the other one's premise, with the same arguments in the same
places. You are shown both at once and asked which direction holds.

They are shown together on purpose. Asked one at a time, a rule like "if X is
part of Y then X is in Y" and its reverse both sound reasonable; side by side
the question is sharper, because at most one of them is usually the one the
passage licenses.

WHAT YOU ARE JUDGING

Only whether each direction is true — as a general claim, or of the particular
things this passage names. You are not judging whether a rule is useful, whether
it helps answer the question, or what the answer is.

  * two expressions being RELATED does not make them equivalent. `part of` and
    `belong to` may both be true of the same pair without either implying the
    other;
  * taxonomy and containment are normally ONE-WAY. Everything in a kingdom is a
    member of it, but not everything that stands in some relation to a kingdom
    is in it; a part implies a whole, the whole does not imply that part;
  * paraphrases and representation variants CAN be two-way. When the two
    predicates are two ways of writing the same thing — a noun and its verb, a
    relation and its nominalisation — both directions may hold;
  * NOT FINDING a counterexample does not make a rule true. If you cannot think
    of one but the direction still looks unsupported, say so;
  * a question asserts nothing. If one of these predicates appears only in the
    question, that tells you what is at issue, not that it is true.

WHAT EACH LINE OF THE INPUT SHOWS

The two rules in full, the guards each carries, whether either mentions a
particular named thing rather than quantifying over everything, and the
sentences the pieces came from.

THE RELATIONS YOU MAY RETURN

  EQUIVALENT          both directions hold
  A_ONLY              A holds; B has a counterexample or is unsupported
  B_ONLY              B holds; A has a counterexample or is unsupported
  CONTEXT_ONLY_BOTH   both hold of the things this passage names, but neither
                      holds generally
  NEITHER             neither direction holds
  UNCERTAIN           you cannot tell from the passage and ordinary knowledge

HOW TO ANSWER

You may think in prose first. Then end with exactly these four lines and nothing
after them:

RELATION: A_ONLY
COUNTEREXAMPLE_A: none
COUNTEREXAMPLE_B: a wheel is part of a car, but a car does not belong to a wheel
SCOPE: general

`COUNTEREXAMPLE_A` and `COUNTEREXAMPLE_B` are one concrete case each, or the
word `none` when you have none. `SCOPE` is `general` when your judgement is
about the world at large, or `passage` when it holds only of the things this
passage names. Only those four lines are read.
