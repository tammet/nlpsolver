A translation pipeline turned a short passage into logic, could not answer the
question, and generated candidate rules to bridge the gap. Some of those
candidates were built from parts a reader chose; others were assembled by code
that filled in what the reader left out. You are shown several candidates for the
SAME connection, together, and asked which of them a careful reader would accept.

Every candidate is written as a defeasible rule: `IF ... THEN normally ...`. A
defeasible rule can be overridden later, so it does not have to be true without
exception. It does have to be a claim the passage or the words themselves
support.

WHAT YOU MUST NOT DO

Do not ask whether a rule would let the prover reach an answer. You are not told
what the answer is, and a rule that reaches the answer while saying something
false is the worst outcome here, not the best. Judge only whether the rule says
something a careful reader would accept.

WHAT THE MARKINGS MEAN

Each candidate says which of its parts were named in the original proposal and
which were added by code. Two markings matter especially:

  * a guard added by code is a restriction nobody asked for — it may be exactly
    the restriction the passage implies, or it may be an unrelated atom that
    happened to be available;
  * an UNVERIFIED ATTACHMENT means code decided that a variable in one sentence
    refers to the same thing as a participant in another. Nothing in the logic
    established that. Say so if it changes your judgment.

Scope is also marked: a rule may be written about the particular individuals in
the passage, or generalised to anything of that shape. Generalising is a real
strengthening — judge the rule you are shown.

WHAT THE CONNECTION RESTS ON

Name where each accepted connection comes from, using exactly one of these:

  TEXTUAL_REPRESENTATION
      The passage states or defines it, or the two sides are two renderings of
      one thing the passage says.
  LEXICAL_TAXONOMY
      The words carry it: a kind-of relation, a compound and its head, a
      synonym. It need not be stated in the passage.
  CONVENTIONAL_NOMINALIZATION_OR_ROLE
      The same happening written two conventional ways: an event and its noun,
      or a relation and its converse naming.
  STABLE_BACKGROUND_RELATION
      Reliable knowledge about the world that is not a fact about the words.
  SPECULATIVE_CORRELATION
      Often true, not reliable.
  OPEN_WORLD_NEGATIVE
      It rests on something not being mentioned.
  NONE
      Nothing supports it.

HOW TO ANSWER

Judge each candidate on its own first. Two candidates may both be acceptable,
and all of them may be unacceptable. Then, if one is clearly the best of the
group, say which.

Write a few sentences of reasoning if you want. Then end with one line per
candidate, and nothing after them except an optional BEST line:

VERDICT: A = ADMIT_DEFEASIBLE; evidence=LEXICAL_TAXONOMY; a sloop is a kind of vessel
VERDICT: B = REJECT; evidence=NONE; the participants are swapped, so it says the harbour is in the boat
VERDICT: C = UNCERTAIN; evidence=STABLE_BACKGROUND_RELATION; usually true but the passage does not say it
BEST: A

Use ADMIT_DEFEASIBLE, REJECT or UNCERTAIN exactly. Give one short reason each.
Use the candidate letters exactly as printed. Only those final lines are read.
