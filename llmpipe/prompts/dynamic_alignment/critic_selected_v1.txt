A logic pipeline could not answer the problem below. Another pass has already
picked a handful of pairs out of its logic, each of the form

    something the theory already has  ->  something a rule or the question needs

Most of those picks are ordinary steps the prover can already take, or simply
beside the point. At most one or two expose a real problem: two expressions the
English treats as connected that the logic wrote as unrelated, or a place where
the logic does not say what the sentence says.

Your job is to sort them out. You are not told any expected answer, no prover
has been run, and nothing you say is judged by whether it makes anything
provable.

Work through these, in ordinary language:

1. Which selected pair, if any, exposes a representation mismatch — a genuine
   gap between what the English says and what the logic says? Name it by its
   id. If none does, say so plainly.
2. Which of the selected pairs are ordinary proof steps the logic already
   supports, or irrelevant? Name each by id and say which it is.
3. What exact connection or correction is needed? State it as a direction,
   from the thing the theory has to the thing something needs, in the terms the
   sentences use.
4. What kind of change is it?
     strict             it always holds
     defeasible         it normally holds but has exceptions
     conditional        it holds only under a stated condition, which you name
     translation repair the logic simply mis-renders the sentence and should be
                        rewritten rather than connected to anything

Be willing to answer "none". A pipeline that invents a connection where the
English does not support one is worse than one that leaves the problem
unsolved.

Answer in exactly this form:

VERDICT: KEEP | REVISE | CONNECT | ALTERNATIVES | UNCERTAIN
MISMATCH: <the id that exposes the mismatch, or "none">
ORDINARY: <ids that are ordinary steps or irrelevant, with a word each>
CONNECTION: <the needed direction or correction, in the sentences' own terms>
KIND: strict | defeasible | conditional | translation repair | none
EDIT REQUEST: <plain-language instruction to whoever will change the logic>

For KEEP and UNCERTAIN write "none" as the EDIT REQUEST.
