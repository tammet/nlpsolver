ACTION-AWARE STAGE 1 — REVISION I

These rules extend the preceding ordinary Stage-1 instructions. Where they
conflict, these explicit overrides take precedence. Return the same JSON list
of sentence packages, each with the exact original sentence in raw and its
semantic units in units. Produce no logic and solve no planning problem.

1. Keep type, add a reading

Keep type real, situation, strict_rule, normal_rule or query. Do not use
instruction or askplan as a type. Add action_reading only when appropriate:

availability: a source presents an available physical operation, including
an explicit denial of that operation. A sufficient conditional rule belongs
here. It is completed by the physical profile, not a promise that all
physical conditions already hold. A sentence that says who cannot do an
operation is availability (a denial), also with a condition on who:
"Toddlers cannot ride the ferry", "A sailor who is not certified cannot
travel by ship".
restriction: a necessary condition on executing an operation: only if,
cannot unless, or requires. Preserve which action and actors it restricts.
effect: a general statement of what an operation changes, possibly under an
old-state condition. This does not grant permission to perform it. An effect
needs an operation that an actor performs. A rule between two states ("If the
jar is sealed, the jar is airtight") is no effect: it has no action_reading
and no actions array.
occurrence: an actual reported action, with its own local event reading.
plan_question: asks for an action sequence achieving the full stated goal.
reachable_question: asks whether some sequence can achieve the goal.
executable_question: asks whether a specified operation is executable now.
verify_question: supplies an ordered hypothetical sequence and asks about
its execution and/or the resulting state.

These are the only action_reading values. State facts need no reading. An
ordinary skill, intention, obligation, speech act, manner or explanation
question need not acquire one. "Birds can fly" and "Can Ada swim?" remain
ordinary capabilities in an ordinary passage. A procedural source specifying
available operations is different; decide from its meaning and discourse,
not from whether a desired plan would need the operation.

2. Preserve the distinctions

"If red, the robot can take the block" is availability. "The robot can take
the block only if red" is restriction. "Taking the block makes the hand
nonempty" is effect. "The robot took the block" is occurrence. "Robo is
holding the key" is the state relation holding: no actions array, no reading. "The robot
should take the block" expresses advice or obligation, not availability.
A public service or route ("A bus service goes from A to B", "There is a
train service from A to B", "A ferry service runs from A to B") is an
ordinary fact: no actions array and no action_reading. availability needs an
actor the sentence names or quantifies ("Mira can travel from A to B by bus",
"Every robot can travel from A to B"); an actor is never borrowed from another
sentence or from the question to fill an empty slot.
Never turn only-if into if, a negative into absence, or a possibility into
an occurrence. Never invent a missing permission, actor, condition or effect.
A sentence that gives a permission and then its result ("A person with the
kettle can brew the tea; then the tea is strong") is two units: the
permission (availability) and the effect.

Choose each action's mode locally. The preceding instructions to synchronize
modes across equal verb roots (the sentence in section 6.2 and section 6.3)
are replaced: can press, pressed, did not press, intends to press and pressing
changes a property retain their different local meanings.

Do not move a rule or hypothetical action into a narrative successor world.
An effect description is not an actual event. A supplied verification
sequence is not an assertion that its steps occurred. Preserve actual events
as occurrences even when the action compiler will report them unsupported.

3. Participants, conditions and scope

Use the existing actions array, natural verb root and roles. Identify actor,
target, source, destination, location, location_prep, instrument and result
when stated. A support or container goes in location with location_prep on or
in; a travel means goes in instrument. Travel by a means is travel, whatever
the verb: "take the bus" and "ride the ferry" have the means as instrument and
no target. Do not create roles named means, support or container. Do not replace the natural verb with a formal constructor.
Every role referencing an entity uses its existing ID. A travel means such
as bus is a lexical value; a pan or whisk used as a tool is an entity. The
instrument comes only from "with X" or "in X" on the action phrase itself: a
possession condition ("If Nia has the pan, Nia can paint the fence") is no
instrument.
A compound such as robot hand keeps the whole compound in its ID
("robot hand 1"); its head noun still says what it is. One entity keeps one
ID: after "There is a delivery van", a later "the van" is delivery van 1.
Keep negation and its scope in text, never as a role. Include the complete
conditional in one unit. Do not add precondition/effect formula arrays.

Generic participants keep generic entity IDs and scope; named participants
keep concrete IDs. Put the IDs in normalized text, as in the ordinary examples;
raw stays verbatim. Keep the ordinary confidence annotation conventions and
preserve all probability words and numbers in text. The action Stage-2 variant
handles the ordinary indefinite-article 0.99 convention. Normally is normal_rule;
can still has mode capability, even in a normal_rule. A named actor's unqualified stated action
rule is strict_rule; a genuine default is normal_rule. An if-rule without
normally, usually or a probability word is strict_rule with no confidence.

State rules follow one convention. A marker decides: "initially", "at the
start" and "now" give a description of the start; "always", "at all times",
"at every state" and "whenever" give a standing rule. Without a marker, a
dependency between states is a standing rule ("If a sack is on the cart,
the sack is loaded"), and a description of the scene is a description of
the start ("Every sack on the cart is dusty", "Nothing is on any sack").
The words if and every do not decide alone. A standing rule is strict_rule
(normal_rule with normally); a description of the start is situation. Keep
the marker words in text. "Every sack on the cart is dusty" does not say
that lifting a sack removes the dust.

4. Questions

This first English action route accepts at most one question sentence; multiple
independent questions receive a validation diagnostic. Formal input can already
contain multiple queries. Keep one query unit for the entire goal, including conjunctions and shared
participants. A plan goal may name no operation: do not invent an actions
array for "Find a plan with b on a and c on b". "How tall?" is not a plan;
"How did Ann arrive?" asks about an occurrence. "Can Ann eventually reach
Tallinn?" asks reachability; "Can Ann take this bus now?" asks immediate
executability. If the reading really cannot be resolved, report an issue.

For a stated number of actions add step_bound:
  {"comparison":"at_most","count":3}
or {"comparison":"exactly","count":3}.
Count is a nonnegative integer. No search default belongs in Stage 1. Zero
is meaningful. Do not turn shortest/cheapest/fastest into at_most; unsupported
optimization requests must be preserved as an issue.

For a supplied hypothetical sequence, add action_order: "sequence" and
list exactly its supplied actions, in order. Preserve the final question
in text. "With no actions performed, is X ...?" is a verify_question with an
empty actions list and no step_bound; "in exactly zero actions" on a plan or
reachability question is its step_bound. A verify_question never has a
step_bound. An action inside the final question is not another sequence step.
Do not invent an ordering for an unordered conjunction or split the question.

5. Context

Keep pre_state/next_state for source-world references. Start with W0;
explicit snapshots can establish later worlds in source order. A rule or
permission without a snapshot phrase has pre_state W0, also after a later
snapshot sentence. A query unit
has no pre_state or next_state. It names planning_root only when the text
selects a snapshot ("Starting from snapshot W0"); otherwise it has no world
field at all, and the compiler selects the root. No inferred
successor is introduced for availability, restriction, effect or verification.

A qualified fact may carry context with the explicitly given entries:
  location: the qualifying location's entity ID;
  location_role: provenance (where the report was made) or scope (where the
    assertion applies);
  knower: the named viewpoint's entity ID.
Use the existing time field for present/past/future. When time contains a
specific expression, preserve it and use state_tense for its relative tense.
Do not duplicate tense in context. Omit unstated entries. "Ann is in Tallinn" puts Tallinn in the assertion,
not in context.location. Never resolve yesterday to the present or invent
a named past snapshot. Preserve temporal or attitude meanings the simple
record cannot express as an issue. A dated permission such as "On Monday Ann
can travel" must retain that scope; it is not an unrestricted rule. If old
location/mental_holder fields are present too, do not equate a mental holder
with a knower unless the sentence actually selects that viewpoint. Conflicting
annotations need correction.

On a query use planning_root, ambient and knower only for an explicitly or
unambiguously selected snapshot, scope location and viewpoint. No fabricated
defaults. These select a view; they do not change the source facts.

6. Do not hide unresolved procedural meaning

Override the command normalization in ordinary section 3.1 for direct requests:
"Find/Give/Show a plan ..." remains a query, never "You should find ...".
"How can ..." is a plan question only when it asks for a sequence to a goal.
A direct request "Find a plan to put b on a" is a plan query. Bare "Put b
on a" may request execution, a plan, or give a procedure step. If unresolved,
retain it and add action_issue:
  {"kind":"ambiguous","text":"Put b on a","detail":"The request does
    not distinguish execution, a plan, or a procedure step."}
Use kind unsupported when the requested interpretation is clear but outside
this first interface, such as an optimization request or a procedure that
cannot be represented without losing scope. Keep its original content.
An issue is metadata, not a new ASU type and not a logical assertion.

An occurrence alone will stay on the ordinary route. Availability,
restriction, effect or a planning-question reading selects the whole passage
for action translation. Therefore annotate readings faithfully even when
the fragment cannot execute them. Never rewrite unsupported movement,
transfer, creation or consumption to a familiar operator merely to fit.
